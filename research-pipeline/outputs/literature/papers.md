## The Impact of Subscription Programs on Customer Purchases
- **Authors**: Raghuram Iyengar, Young-Hoon Park, Qi Yu
- **Year**: 2022
- **Journal**: Journal of Marketing Research, Vol. 59(6), pp. 1101–1119
- **Citations**: 38+
- **Abstract summary**: Uses difference-in-differences combined with generalized random forests to estimate individual-level treatment effects of a subscription program on customer purchases. Finds a large, persistent, heterogeneous increase in purchases following subscription adoption.
- **Relevance**: The most rigorous causal study on how subscription programs change customer purchase behavior. Directly relevant to the restaurant context.
- **Key finding**: Only one-third of the purchase increase is due to economic benefits (price savings); two-thirds is a non-economic behavioral effect — consistent with sunk-cost and commitment mechanisms.

## Sunk Cost Effect, Self-Control, and Contract Design
- **Authors**: Xing Zhang, Ganesh Iyer, Xiaoyan Xu, Juin Kuan Chong
- **Year**: 2024
- **Journal**: Journal of Marketing Research
- **Citations**: ~10
- **Abstract summary**: Models how consumers strategically anticipate the sunk cost effect and use upfront fees as a self-control commitment device. Analyzes how firms should optimally design contracts given this behavioral dynamic.
- **Relevance**: Provides the theoretical micro-foundation for why restaurant subscriptions may increase visit frequency beyond what economic value alone predicts — consumers use the fee to commit themselves to visit.
- **Key finding**: Sunk costs have a nonmonotonic effect on firm profits under fixed-fee contracts; the sunk cost effect serves dual roles as distortion (inflexibility) and commitment device.

## The Flat-Rate Pricing Paradox: Conflicting Effects of "All-You-Can-Eat" Buffet Pricing
- **Authors**: [Authors not fully confirmed in search; published in Review of Economics and Statistics]
- **Year**: 2011
- **Journal**: Review of Economics and Statistics, Vol. 93(1), pp. 193–200
- **Citations**: High (foundational paper)
- **Abstract summary**: Uses a randomized experiment in an all-you-can-eat pizza restaurant where price was varied. Finds mixed evidence — higher price conditions did not consistently produce greater consumption, contradicting simple sunk-cost predictions.
- **Relevance**: The closest empirical analog to restaurant subscription consumption behavior. Establishes that flat-rate pricing in restaurants does not mechanically increase consumption — consumer heterogeneity matters.
- **Key finding**: AYCE buffet pricing produces conflicting effects; higher-price consumers sometimes rated food less tasty and consumed less, suggesting the relationship between price and consumption is non-linear.

## Pricing and Consumption in Subscription Settings
- **Authors**: Pedro Gardete, Daniela Maria Schmitt, Florian Stahl
- **Year**: 2025
- **Journal**: Nova SBE Working Paper Series No. 674 / SSRN (forthcoming)
- **Citations**: Working paper
- **Abstract summary**: Examines how subscription pricing affects usage intensity using data from an online news publisher with promotional pricing variation. Accounts for selection and heterogeneity in churn behavior.
- **Relevance**: Most recent empirical investigation of the price–consumption relationship in subscription settings. Methodological lessons transferable to restaurant subscriptions.
- **Key finding**: Promotional (lower-price) subscribers consume substantially more than regular-price subscribers even after controlling for churn — suggests consumption is driven by non-price factors.

## The Managed Heart: Commercialization of Human Feeling
- **Authors**: Arlie Russell Hochschild
- **Year**: 1983
- **Journal**: Book — University of California Press (won Charles Cooley Award, ASA)
- **Citations**: Extremely high (foundational)
- **Abstract summary**: Introduces the concept of emotional labor — the management of feeling to create a publicly observable facial and bodily display. Documents how service workers (flight attendants, restaurant servers) are required to manage and commercialize their emotions.
- **Relevance**: The theoretical backbone for studying employee wellbeing in high-contact service environments like restaurants. Any study of subscription-driven increases in customer frequency must account for emotional labor demands.
- **Key finding**: Commercializing emotion leads to worker estrangement from genuine feeling; "surface acting" (faking emotion) is more damaging than "deep acting" (genuinely modifying internal state).

## Emotional Energy: When Customer Interactions Energize Service Employees
- **Authors**: Julien Cayla, Brigitte Auriacombe
- **Year**: 2025
- **Journal**: Journal of Marketing, Vol. (forthcoming 2025)
- **Citations**: Early-stage
- **Abstract summary**: Challenges the dominant burnout framing by documenting conditions under which customer interactions create positive emotional energy for service workers.
- **Relevance**: Provides a counterpoint — not all increases in customer frequency harm employee wellbeing. Subscription-driven regulars may actually energize servers through relationship formation.
- **Key finding**: Repeated positive interactions with regular customers can build "emotional energy" that sustains worker engagement; relationship regularity matters.
